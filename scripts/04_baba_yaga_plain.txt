If you ever find a hut in the forest standing on chicken legs… don't knock. Deep in the forests of Russia, every child knows the name of the old woman who lives there. They call her Baba Yaga. The witch with a bone leg and teeth of iron. She doesn't walk. She flies through the night in a giant mortar, steering with a pestle… and sweeping away her tracks with a broom. Her hut stands on chicken legs. It turns, and turns… so no one can find the door. Around it stands a fence made of bones. And on every post… a human skull. At night, the eyes of the skulls begin to glow. In the oldest story, a girl named Vasilisa was sent to her hut to fetch fire. To get inside, she had to say the words. Little hut, little hut… turn your back to the forest, and your front to me. The hut groaned… and slowly turned toward her. The door opened. Baba Yaga gave her impossible tasks. If she failed even one… she would be eaten. Vasilisa survived, thanks to a little doll her dying mother gave her. Most others never left. So if you're ever lost in the woods, and you hear wood creaking… like something huge is turning toward you… don't say a word. She can smell you.
